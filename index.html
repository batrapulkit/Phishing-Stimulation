Know your enemy

Six phishing attacks you'll meet in real life

Every one of these is in the challenge.

🎭

Look-alike domains

nlmbuslabs.com instead of nimbuslabs.com, or nimbuslabs-helpdesk.com. One wrong letter is all it takes.

👔

CEO / boss fraud

An "executive" on a personal email needs gift cards or an urgent transfer, and can't take calls.

🧾

Invoice fraud

An overdue invoice with "updated bank details" and an attachment that isn't really a PDF.

📁

Fake file shares

"A colleague shared Bonus_Ratings.xlsx." The sign-in page is a trap built to steal your password.

🏦

Spoofed bank alerts

The From address looks perfect because it's forged. Only the security checks and the link reveal it.

💸

Payroll scams

"Your salary is on hold, re-enter your bank details." Timed for the end of the month.

Red flags checklist

6 checks before you click anything

Screenshot this. It catches most attacks.

01

Read the real domain

Ignore the display name. Read the address after the @, letter by letter. nlmbuslabs ≠ nimbuslabs.

02

Check where links really go

Link text can say anything. Hover or long-press to see the true destination; read the domain right to left.

03

Urgency means slow down

"2 hours", "final notice", "salary on hold". Pressure is designed to stop you thinking.

04

Money or passwords? Verify separately

Call or message the person on a number or channel you already trust, never one from the email.

05

Suspicious attachments

.html files, password-protected .zip files and "enable macros" documents are classic payloads.

06

Never share OTPs or card details

No real bank, employer or support team asks for them by email, SMS or phone. Ever.

FAQ

Questions

Is this safe to play?

Yes. Every email, company, person and domain is fictional. No link or attachment in the emails opens. We only store the name, email and phone number you enter, plus your final score.

Why do you ask for my details?

So we can send you your result and follow up with cybersecurity training and resources. Your details are never sold or shared. Nothing to install: it runs in your browser on any phone or laptop.

Why are some emails real?

Because real inboxes are mostly real email. If you report everything, you ignore genuine alerts and slow your team down. Good security is about judging accurately, not panicking.

What are SPF, DKIM and DMARC?

Checks that confirm whether an email really came from the domain it claims. A fail is a strong warning sign. A pass only proves the sender owns that domain, which an attacker can do with a look-alike domain.

I clicked a real phishing link. What now?

Change your password immediately from the real website, change it anywhere you reused it, and report it to your IT or security team straight away. Speed matters more than embarrassment.
